TLS

Connectivity in NiCE CXone requires at least TLS 1.2.

Inbound, from CXone to your servers, must support at least one of: TLS_DHE_RSA_WITH_AES_128_GCM_SHA256 (0x009E), TLS_ECDHE_RSA_WITH_AES_128_CBC_SHA (0xC013), TLS_ECDHE_ECDSA_WITH_AES_128_GCM_SHA256 (0xC02B), TLS_ECDHE_ECDSA_WITH_AES_256_GCM_SHA384 (0xC02C).

Outbound, from your servers to CXone, must support at least one of: TLS_ECDHE_RSA_WITH_AES_128_GCM_SHA256 (0xC02F) and TLS_ECDHE_RSA_WITH_AES_256_GCM_SHA384 (0xC030). The page recommends both.

Keys and people

An access key belongs to an employee and inherits that employee’s permissions. The secret is shown once. Disabling the key kills tokens.

User-facing apps in this lab follow the authorization-code flow. The authentication pages also show a password grant for back-end integrations, where the username is the access key id.

External IdP is a documented setup, not the default this page assumes you have.

FedRAMP is an environment, not a slogan

The connectivity requirements page describes FedRAMP as isolated from commercial operations, with continuous monitoring and restricted access. The API getting-started page names a separate issuer, https://cxone-gov.niceincontact.com.

This campus does not claim HIPAA, PCI, SOC 2, or a particular encryption-at-rest setting. Those sentences are not in the passages gathered here.
